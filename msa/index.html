All the Photographs of Flamboyant Trees I Find on the Internet

This series begins with an impossible action: to paint every photograph of a flamboyant tree that I find on the Internet. The word “every” produces a potentially infinite archive, and I am faced with an endless task.

In Cuba, my home country, repetition has been used to fix political images, symbols, and ideas until they become familiar and unquestionable. My procedure starts from this same logic, but reverses it: to repeat in order to erode. By returning again and again to the same motif, what initially appears as an ornamental image begins to lose its stability.

This gesture also has a personal origin. I grew up working in our family workshop, producing paintings intended for the tourist market. My father belongs to a generation that learned to accept or resign itself to the legacy of Cuba’s communist system; my generation confronts it through dissatisfaction. Painting over the flamboyant tree thus becomes a way of confronting an inherited image, something that is also part of my own inheritance.

I do not seek to completely erase the image, but to make it recede: to gradually take away its luminosity and allow other layers to emerge. The impossibility of painting every image is what keeps the work open. Each new photograph prolongs the task, and each new painting calls the previous one into question.

The flamboyant tree remains, but its image can no longer remain intact.

Mario Sergio Alvarez
